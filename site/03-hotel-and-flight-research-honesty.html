3. Hotel and Flight Research Honesty

The uncomfortable truth about AI and prices

AI chat assistants are generally not connected to live booking systems. When you ask "what's a good hotel in Lisbon under $120?", you get: (a) hotels the AI remembers from its training data — sometimes years stale, (b) prices that are either made up, long-gone, or suspiciously round — and (c) reviews that may not reflect this year's management.

This is not because the AI is lazy. It's because prices and availability are facts that change by the minute, and most chatbots can't or don't look them up live. Honesty about this limitation is the feature: once you accept that AI research is a shortlist generator, not a booking engine, everything works.

The role split, in one table

If AI ever quotes you a live-sounding price, say: "Do you have live access to that price, or is it from recollection?" Watch what it answers. Use that information accordingly.

Method: shortlist → live check → book direct

Step 1 — Shortlist with a sharp prompt:

I need a hotel in [CITY], [DATES], budget about [USD] per night,
for [TRAVELERS]. I care about: [quiet room, breakfast, walkable to X,
late check-in, ...]. Suggest a shortlist of 5 candidates and tell me
one reason to pick and one reason to doubt each. If you're not sure
a hotel still operates or has been renovated recently, say so.
Then list anything I need to confirm on a booking site.

Step 2 — Live check, 5 minutes: Search each shortlisted name in a major booking site or metasearch. Confirm: still operating, rate in your budget, rooms actually available on your dates, free cancellation (worth a small premium when plans are soft).

Step 3 — Reviews yourself, 5 minutes: Sort reviews newest-first. Read the 5 most recent and the 5 lowest recent ones. Patterns matter more than scores: "thin walls" in three 2024–2025 reviews is real; one 2017 rant is noise. Then ask AI:

Here are snippets from recent guest reviews. Which complaints are
one-off bad luck and which are structural, based on how many
independent guests mention them?

Step 4 — Book direct (usually): Once you've chosen, the hotel's own site (or the airline's own site) often matches agency prices but with easier cancellations, better room picks, and a contact that can actually help you if plans change. Booking direct also removes an entire layer of third-party confusion — which chapter 6 cares about a lot.

Flights: what AI is actually useful for

  • Route and hub strategy: "cheapest realistic way from [A] to [B]: direct, self-transfer, or through which hub? What seasons change the answer?"
  • Timing wisdom: midweek departures, how early to arrive for a connection, why a 3-hour connection is safer than a 1-hour one on separate tickets.
  • Separate-ticket danger check — very useful, very important: if you plan to book two separate tickets to save money, AI can gut-check them:
I want to book flight A (arrives [time]) and a separate cheaper
flight B (departs [time]) from the same or connected airports. You DID
NOT book these. Advise minimal safe connection time for self-transfer
with checked bags at [AIRPORT], and tell me the risks if flight A is late.
  • What AI cannot do: see today's fares. Genuine search happens on metasearch sites and airline websites — AI just tells you what to search for and what traps to avoid (e.g., why "budget" airfares at [cheap hub] can include fees that eliminate savings).

The honesty habits

  1. Assume any price the AI says is at best a year old, at worst invented — always re-price on a live site. An honestly-expressed year-old ballpark is fine for understanding; an invented "cheap" number is not something to book.
  2. Ask for the doubts, not the selling point. Whenever AI proposes a hotel, airline, or neighborhood, follow up with: "Give me the strongest argument against this."
  3. Never paste booking references, passport data, or full itineraries into a chat. AI doesn't need them; scammers in chat would.
  4. Beware the AI that says "prices usually rise, book now through this link." Genuine chat tools don't have links to book through, and any pressure to "book now" in a chat is a red flag (see chapter 6).

Copy-ready prompts

Take my shortlist of 5 hotels and rank them by how well they fit these
constraints, then tell me which single fact could disqualify each one
and has to be verified live.

Be genuinely critical: what's the strongest argument AGAINST booking
the hotel you recommended?
