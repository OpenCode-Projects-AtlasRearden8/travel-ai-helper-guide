5. Offline Planning with Local Models

The dead-zone problem

National parks, mountain trains, ferries, foreign basements, airplane seats — some of the best parts of a trip happen where the internet doesn't. Cloud AI tools go dark exactly when you need to re-plan: a closed trailhead, a missed connection, "where can we eat nearby?" (which needs internet, but "what do we do now that it's raining?" does not).

The fix is a local model: a small AI that runs entirely on your own laptop (or, these days, some phones). No internet, no account, no data leaving the machine. This chapter is deliberately beginner-friendly — you can do this even if you've never installed anything but a browser.

What "local model" means in one paragraph

Big-name AI chatbots run on company servers; your phone sends text to them and gets text back. A local model is a much smaller AI file that runs on your hardware with a small app. Modern small models (7–8 billion-parameter class) are genuinely useful for this manual's tasks: itineraries, packing lists, translations, re-planning when plans break. They are weaker than cloud models at current-events facts — which doesn't matter, because half your planning is already done before you board the plane.

Important: local models are still not fact-checkers. Everything in the verification checklist applies — the model just doesn't need wifi to be wrong.

Step 1: Install a free local AI app

A standard beginner setup today (all free, no account):

  1. Choose a runner app. Popular beginner-friendly options include Ollama (command-line style but simple, runs on most laptops) and LM Studio (point-and-click GUI, downloads models inside the app). For most people, LM Studio is the gentler first stop.
  2. Download one small model. Inside the app, pick a well-known small instruct model — think "a modern 7–8B class model" (names like Llama 3.1 8B Instruct, Qwen 2.5 7B Instruct, Mistral 7B Instruct are common families). Size on disk: about 4–6 GB.
  3. Test offline. Turn off your wifi and chat with it. If it answers, you're ready for dead zones.

Hardware reality check: 8 GB of RAM is workable, 16 GB is comfortable. A 5-year old laptop may be slow but usable. If it's painfully slow, use a smaller quantized model — the app will show you options.

Step 2: The flight plan — do the cloud work at home

Local models shine when you pre-load the trip into them before you leave. At home, on fast wifi, do this with your cloud AI, then carry the results:

  • Chapter 1's itinerary draft, verified.
  • Chapter 2's screenshot deck of translated sentences.
  • Chapter 4's packing list.
  • The trip worksheet filled in.

Then, in a dead zone, the local model's job is re-planning, not re-searching:

Here is my day plan (itinerary below) and today's constraints
(trailhead closed / rain / missed 9:12 train / illness at 2 p.m.).
Full itinerary: [PASTE]. Constraint: [DESCRIBE].
Re-plan only the affected day, staying walkable from here, using
the assumption that nothing new can be verified online right now.
Mark anything that needs verification done later.

Copy the finished itinerary into your notes app before you travel, and paste it into the local model — local models have no memory of what you did at home. The paste is the memory.

Step 3 (optional): A private reference card for sensitive moments

One genuine advantage of local models: nothing you type leaves your machine. A useful, safe pattern for booking-sensitive moments:

Here are my options, written as [A with reason] / [B with reason] / [C with reason].
Help me think through which to pick, using only what I've given you.
No personal data is included, and I will make the final decision myself.

One caution worth keeping: never paste passwords, payment card numbers, full passport numbers, or actual ticket codes into any AI — even a purely local one — out of pure habit discipline. It costs you nothing and keeps the rule simple.

Step 4: The offline day-trip script

A practical combo that works without any signal:

  1. Maps offline — most major map apps allow pre-downloading regions. Download your destination city and its airport region at home.
  2. Translations offline (chapter 2, layer 1).
  3. Local model — for the "we're stuck, advise me" conversations.
  4. Paper backup — one printed page (like the trip worksheet): day plan on paper fits in a pocket and never needs a charger.

What local models are bad at (manage expectations)

  • Anything time-sensitive: it doesn't know today's date, opening hours, or that a museum burned down last month.
  • Foreign-language breadth: cloud translation beats small local models for uncommon languages; rely on the offline translation apps for that, and use the local model for logistics reasoning, not translation.
  • Long documents: small models can lose track on very long pasted text — keep pastes short and specific.

Setup checklist (print this bit)

  • Runner app installed and tested offline.
  • One small model downloaded (~4–6 GB).
  • Airplane-mode chat test passed at home.
  • Itinerary + worksheet + translation deck saved in notes app.
  • Offline maps downloaded for destination and transit regions.
  • Laptop + charger + (optional) power bank packed.
